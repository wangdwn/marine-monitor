# Global Marine Industry "Application Scenario" Building — Case Research for Guangzhou Marine-Economy Planning

**Mission:** How countries turn a marine scenario from an opportunity list into a verifiable, tradable, replicable project. For a Guangzhou (China) marine-economy planning report, alongside Guangzhou's 2027 marine projects (deep-pit test site, seabed survey, offshore wind, marine monitoring data, smart ports).
**Research date:** 2026-11-07
**Scope:** Main countries: Norway, Singapore, United Kingdom, Japan, South Korea, Netherlands. United States and Australia only as supplementary mentions, no separate chapters.
**Scenario types (5):** (1) deep-sea equipment and test sites, (2) offshore wind and ocean energy, (3) smart ports and shipping, (4) marine biopharmaceuticals / marine biotechnology, (5) marine data and monitoring services. Excluded as main types: fisheries/aquaculture, coastal tourism.
**Method / source rule:** Official government releases, official scenario/opportunity lists, industry white papers, public financial reports and company announcements only. No social-media / self-media claims. Source name, publication date and URL are given for every case and every key figure. Any of the four elements not verifiable from a primary source is marked **"not verified"**. Figures from a single source are flagged **single-source**.
**Verification status:** Most primary pages were read 2026-11-07 via `browser_open` (crawled <1 minute before reading for Equinor, Yara, Smart Sound Plymouth, MARIN, Kystverket/BarentsWatch, MPA, RWE, Seabed 2030, JPMA/JAMSTEC, PMO Singapore) — labelled **verified live (index fetch)** below. The research-browser window itself failed on the one live attempt (MPA Tuas page, tab `page_gone`, no tabs on re-list) — labelled where relevant; no fact in this report rests on that failed live attempt alone. Other facts are labelled **index** (search-result text, not a full page open).

## Summary

**Answer, in one paragraph:** Fifteen named, verifiable cases — exactly 3 per scenario type — were assembled across the six main countries (Norway 4, UK 3, Singapore 2, Japan 2, South Korea 2, Netherlands 2). The pattern that repeats is not a single "scenario list": it is a **four-party contract** — (a) a named owner/issuer who offers a real site, route or resource, (b) a named payer (usually a state grant/auction, not a vague subsidy), (c) a named validator (a ministry inspection, a classification society, an ISO/GMP system, or a CfD milestone — and where no validator could be found from a primary source, that is stated), and (d) a named first buyer/user who takes the output on a real route, grid or production line. The strongest, most replicable models for Guangzhou are: **Hywind Tampen** (captive first buyer — oil platforms), **Dogger Bank / Borssele** (seabed lease + price auction + grid by the state, in that order), **MEGURI2040 GENBU** (foundation finance + ministry writes the rule from trial data, 4 vessel types in parallel), **KRISO / MARIN deep basins** (a priced, bookable deep-pit test service — the closest analogue to Guangzhou's deep-pit test site), **JAMSTEC's Deep-sea Bio Open Innovation Platform** (low-cost resource library, user owns IP on derivatives), and **BarentsWatch / digitalPORT@SG / BODC-Seabed 2030** (one national data portal, open APIs, multi-agency reuse). Marine biotech is the thinnest type: no counted case has a marketed marine-derived drug with a named pharmaceutical buyer; the three biotech cases are enzymes, a resource platform, and a biohydrogen demo — all pre-commercial or B2B-ingredient stage. That thinness is itself a finding, recorded in §5 Gaps.

**Confidence:** High for the existence, issuer, payer and first-user of the wind, smart-shipping and test-site cases (primary company/government releases). Medium for exact validation/certification bodies in several cases (marked "not verified"). Low for any single marine-economy size figure — definitions differ by country and most figures are single-source (flagged in §3).

## 1. Country mechanisms — how scenarios are published, matched, piloted, procured

### 1.1 Norway
- **Publish / offer:** Ministry of Energy allocates offshore-wind areas and runs tenders (e.g. Utsira Nord floating-wind tender: qualitative phase 1 allocated sites to Equinor/Vårgrønn and EDF/Deep Wind Offshore in Dec 2025, then a sealed-bid phase 2 for up to NOK 35bn state support; Enova separately launched a NOK 10bn small-scale floating-wind competition, bids to 12 Feb 2026 — Recharge, 11 Dec 2025, via search, index). The Norwegian Maritime Authority + Norwegian Coastal Administration signed an agreement in 2016 establishing test areas for autonomous ships — described as the first of their kind in the world (MDPI, 31 Oct 2026, index).
- **Pay:** Enova (state enterprise, Ministry of Climate and Environment) makes large, named, project-specific grants (Hywind Tampen NOK 2.3bn; Yara Birkeland up to NOK 133.5m). The Business Sector's NOx Fund co-pays emission-reduction projects (NOK 566m for Hywind Tampen).
- **Pilot / validate:** Ministry approval of Plans for Development and Operation (PDO); EFTA Surveillance Authority (ESA) state-aid approval (Hywind Tampen, 3 Dec 2020, highest individual aid award then approved — index). For autonomous ships, a multi-phase NMA/DNV framework is described in secondary sources; the primary Yara release only says "two-year testing period … finally certified as an autonomous … ship" — certifier **not verified** from that primary release.
- **Procure / data:** BarentsWatch development work "is carried out by commercial suppliers through public procurement" (Kystverket, verified live). Ten ministries + 29 agencies/institutes are partners.

### 1.2 Singapore
- **Publish / offer:** MPA runs themed **Calls for Proposals (CFP)** with a named testbed (the port itself) and named test-bedding partners (shipowners). Example, verified live: MPA, undated 2026 release, "MPA Selects Six Partners for In-Water Hull Inspection and Cleaning Innovation Trials" — 19 proposals from 36 companies across 9 countries; 6 partners selected.
- **Pay:** Maritime Innovation and Technology (MINT) Fund, established 2003 — MINT-RPD for applied R&D / product development / test-bedding, and MINT-STARTUP up to SGD 100,000 for proof-of-concept for PIER71 alumni (Estonia MFA / Orissa-Intralink report, Jan 2025, index; MPA CFP FAQ, 2025, index). The hull-cleaning CFP: close to SGD 3.7m R&D co-funding for 6 partners, trials H2 2026 over 18 months (MPA, verified live).
- **Pilot / validate:** Trials in live port conditions, with classification societies and shipowners validating "under actual operating conditions" (MPA, verified live). PIER71 (MPA + NUS) is the startup accelerator; MPA Living Lab is the co-innovation/testbed arm.
- **Procure / scale:** Sea Transport Industry Transformation Map (ITM) 2018, refreshed ITM 2025 (launched SMW 2022) — Tuas Port as "largest fully automated container terminal", 1,000 additional good local jobs, net-zero ports by 2050 (PMO Singapore, SMW 2022 speech, verified live). digitalPORT@SG (2019) → digitalOCEANS (2020, data-standard harmonisation) → OCEANS-X (2022, API marketplace).

### 1.3 United Kingdom
- **Publish / offer:** The Crown Estate leases seabed (Round 3 zones, incl. Dogger Bank; Round 4 agreements for lease, e.g. Dogger Bank South Jan 2023). DESNZ runs Contracts for Difference (CfD) allocation rounds; the Planning Inspectorate examines Development Consent Orders (DCO).
- **Pay:** CfD — a 15-year guaranteed strike price, auctioned. AR3 (20 Sep 2019, gov.uk, index): Dogger Bank A £39.65/MWh, B and C £41.61/MWh (2012 prices), 1,200MW each. AR7 (Jan 2026): Dogger Bank South £91.20/MWh (RWE, 14 May 2026, verified live). Low Carbon Contracts Company (LCCC) administers CfDs and a milestone requirement (10% spend / commitments).
- **Pilot / validate:** Smart Sound Plymouth (PML) offers authorised water space, ROC, 5G and digital twins "for autonomy assurance & regulation"; Innovate UK / research-grant / commercial budgets are the funding routes named on its own site (verified live). For marine data, NERC's Environmental Data Service designates BODC as the marine data centre.
- **Procure:** Defence procurement readiness is explicit at Smart Sound (MoD clearance, Cyber Essentials Plus, JOSCAR/Aurora/Helios — Smart Sound site, verified live).

### 1.4 Japan
- **Publish / offer:** 4th Basic Plan on Ocean Policy adopted by Cabinet Apr 2023 (2023–2028), coordinated by Cabinet Office Headquarters for Ocean Policy across MLIT, METI, MOE, MAFF (Vietnam.vn summary citing Cabinet Office 2023, index). Offshore wind under the 2019 Act on Promotion of Use of Sea Areas for Renewable Energy: bidding rounds for 30-year sea-use rights; targets 10GW by 2030, 30–45GW by 2040, incl. floating ≥15GW.
- **Pay:** NEDO Green Innovation Fund (¥2 trillion, established 2021) funds floating-wind Phase 2 demonstrations (southern Akita, led by Marubeni Offshore Wind Development; Tahara–Toyohashi, Aichi, led by C-tech — selected Jun 2024; 15MW-class semi-submersibles, commissioning late 2020s) (DeepWind / NEDO, index). The Nippon Foundation is the catalytic payer for MEGURI2040 (amount **not verified** from a primary source — see C9).
- **Pilot / validate:** MLIT writes inspection rules from trial data: 2025 amendments in force 1 Jun 2025, inspection guidance 30 Jun 2025 (role split crew/system, crew override, log ≥1 voyage/48h kept 6 months — OceanTech Intelligence, index, secondary). ClassNK grants AUTO-NAV notations; MLIT then inspects as an autonomous vessel (MASS). JAMSTEC's Deep-sea Bio Open Innovation Platform (2017) is the biotech analogue: trial provision, then full provision Feb 2020.
- **Procure / scale:** Four Stage-2 vessels entered commercial service Dec 2025–Mar 2026 (see C9); the Foundation presented the update to IMO MSC 111 (May 2026) to feed the future mandatory MASS Code (target adoption Jul 2030, entry into force Jan 2032).

### 1.5 South Korea
- **Publish / offer:** Offshore-wind auctions run under the government roadmap; 2026 H1 ceiling prices KRW 171,229/MWh (fixed, mainland), KRW 175,100/MWh (floating, mainland); 1.786GW awarded across 5 projects in Jun 2026 from 3.656GW of bids (NRF/EPAM focus, Jul 2026, index). Ministry of Oceans and Fisheries (MOF) Maritime & Fisheries AI Transformation (AX) Strategy (announced Oct 2026, Herald Corp 28 Oct 2026, index): Level-4 fully unmanned vessel core technology by 2032, top global autonomous-ship market by 2035.
- **Pay:** Not verified in a single primary figure for the counted cases. Offshore-wind developers are KEPCO/generation companies + private consortia; KIOST/KRISO are government institutes (KIOST under MOF).
- **Pilot / validate:** KRISO operates the Deep Ocean Engineering Basin (Busan) and an Autonomous Ship Verification & Evaluation Research Center (Ulsan, completed 3 Nov 2022) (KRISO, verified live). MOF will build an AI-port test facility at Gwangyang by 2029 and validate physical-AI cargo handling there before rollout to Jinhae New Port (Herald Corp, Oct 2026, index — target, not outcome).
- **Procure / scale:** Jinhae New Port (partly opening 2032) is presented as the world's first port designed with AI from the design stage (Korea Maritime Institute, via NoCut News 27 Oct 2026, index). Busan New Port West Container Terminal (Dongwon) opened Apr 2024 as Korea's first fully automated container terminal; KRW 200bn expansion agreed 20 Oct 2026 (see notes/source-02).

### 1.6 Netherlands
- **Publish / offer:** RVO (Netherlands Enterprise Agency) "prepares and issues permits, conducts site studies, coordinates tenders, and publishes them" (RVO Dutch Offshore Wind Innovation Guide 2026, index). Ministry of Infrastructure & Water Management + Ministry of Climate Policy & Green Growth allocate areas; Rijkswaterstaat conducts site studies; TenneT (state TSO) builds the offshore grid.
- **Pay:** SDE++ (formerly SDE+) subsidy auctions, then subsidy-free tenders judged on six quality criteria (Hollandse Kust (zuid) I–II, Dec 2017). Borssele III–IV: winning bid 5.45 €c/kWh (see C6). A dedicated innovation site, Borssele Site V (6–20MW), was tendered Jan 2018 with investment + operating subsidy (CMS Law, 2017, index). 2025 1GW tender received no bids; RVO letter 16 Jan 2026 announces a 1GW subsidy tender for 2026 and a roll-out pace cut from 4GW to 2GW in 2026 (RVO, index).
- **Pilot / validate:** MARIN (Wageningen) is the commercial model-test validator (see C2). Port of Rotterdam Authority aims to enable autonomous navigation in the port by 2030, using a digital twin (port smart-infrastructure page, via Europesays 30 Oct 2026, index).
- **Procure / scale:** Roadmap 21GW (Cabinet decision early 2022; later adjusted to ~2032). North Sea Wind Energy Infrastructure Plan (WIN, Jul 2025) lowered the 2040 target from 50GW to 30–40GW (Brussels Signal, Jul 2025, index).

### 1.7 Supplementary mentions only — United States and Australia (no separate chapters)
- **United States:** First commercial-scale (>100MW) offshore wind farm, South Fork Wind (132MW), full commissioning 14 Mar 2024; Vineyard Wind 1 first power Jan 2024 (Wikipedia summary, index — secondary). US DOE Offshore Wind Market Report 2023: development/operational pipeline 52,687MW to 31 May 2023 (Fordham ELR, index). Mechanism contrast: federal leasing by BOEM + state PPAs, no single national CfD-style auction — **not verified** in a primary source in this run; do not cite as a verified mechanism.
- **Australia:** Integrated Marine Observing System (IMOS), NCRIS-enabled, University of Tasmania as lead agent; all data openly/free via the Australian Ocean Data Network (AODN) portal; >1bn measurements; $63.5m Australian Government boost (IMOS, index). Useful as a marine-data comparator to BODC/BarentsWatch, but no Australian case is counted in the 15.

## 2. Case list — by country, then by scenario type

**How to read each case:** Issuer/offer | Pays/funding | Validation/standards/certification | Final buyer / first user | Guangzhou borrow note. "Not verified" means no primary source found in this run for that element — not that it does not exist.

### 2.1 Norway (4 cases)

#### C1 — Type 2 Offshore wind: Hywind Tampen (floating, 88MW nameplate)
- **Issuer/offer:** Equinor (operator) + Snorre/Gullfaks licence partners; Plans for Development and Operation approved by the Norwegian Ministry of Petroleum and Industry, Apr 2020.
- **Pays/funding:** Enova NOK 2.3bn + Business Sector's NOx Fund NOK 566m. Total investment forecast ~NOK 7.4bn at opening (Aug 2023) vs ~NOK 5bn at PDO. Source: Equinor magazine, read 2026-11-07, verified live — https://www.equinor.com/magazine/olea-and-the-floating-wind-turbines ; Equinor press release 23 Aug 2023 (via search).
- **Validation/standards:** **Not verified** (technical certifier). Regulatory approval only is verified: ministry PDO approval + ESA state-aid approval (3 Dec 2020). No DNV/class-society certificate was named in the primary sources read.
- **Final buyer / first user:** Snorre A/B and Gullfaks A/B/C oil-and-gas platforms — ~35% of their annual power demand; 11 turbines, 140km offshore, 260–300m water. CO2 −200,000t/yr, NOx −1,000t/yr. Owners: Equinor, Petoro, OMV, Vår Energi, Harbour Energy, INPEX Idemitsu Norge.
- **Note on figures:** Equinor's magazine gives total system capacity 94.6MW; earlier releases give 88MW. Flagged discrepancy — do not use a single capacity figure without noting it. **Single-source** for the 94.6MW figure (Equinor magazine).
- **Guangzhou borrow:** Use a captive, creditworthy first buyer (here, offshore platforms) to de-risk a first-of-a-kind floating system, instead of selling into a merchant power market on day one. Enova's grant is tied to a named technology-learning goal, not a generic subsidy.

#### C2 — Type 3 Smart ports/shipping: Yara Birkeland (autonomous, battery-electric container feeder)
- **Issuer/offer:** Yara International (owner/operator) + Kongsberg (autonomy technology) + VARD (builder). Offered as a Porsgrunn–Brevik fertilizer feeder.
- **Pays/funding:** Enova allocated up to NOK 133.5m. Source: Yara corporate release, published 2021-11-19, updated 2025-04-16, verified live — https://www.yara.com/corporate-releases/yara-to-start-operating-the-worlds-first-fully-emission-free-container-ship/?ref=futuretides.org
- **Validation/standards:** Yara release: "two-year testing period … finally certified as an autonomous, all-electric container ship." Certifying body **not verified** from that primary release. Secondary sources describe a Norwegian Maritime Authority + DNV multi-phase MASS framework — **not verified** from a primary source in this run.
- **Final buyer / first user:** Yara itself — transports mineral fertilizer Porsgrunn–Brevik; in commercial operation from 2022; replaces 40,000 diesel truck trips/yr, cuts 1,000t CO2/yr. Operated from Massterly's (Kongsberg + Wilhelmsen JV) monitoring/operations centre, Horten.
- **Guangzhou borrow:** Owner-as-first-user + staged autonomy (manned → remote → unmanned) + a shore operations centre as a deliverable in its own right. The truck-trip figure is a verifiable, auditable outcome metric Guangzhou could copy for a port feeder.

#### C3 — Type 4 Marine biotech: ArcticZymes Technologies ASA (cold-adapted marine enzymes, Tromsø)
- **Issuer/offer:** ArcticZymes Technologies ASA (listed OSE: AZT), Tromsø; bioprospecting in collaboration with UiT — The Arctic University of Norway. Product: recombinant cold-adapted enzymes for molecular research, in-vitro diagnostics (IVD) and biomanufacturing.
- **Pays/funding:** Commercial sales (customers pay). **Not verified:** any government grant amount for ArcticZymes. Company financials: total revenue NOK 118.2m in FY2025 (2024: NOK 108.1m); sales revenue NOK 112.6m (2024: NOK 104.4m). Source: ArcticZymes Q4/FY2025 press release, 12 Feb 2026 — https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/64c25a874bf82d94feb670d6/6a2144d1eec4dcf6ad5e13ad_AZT-Q4-2025-Press-Release.pdf (index). **Single-source** (company release).
- **Validation/standards:** Manufactured under ISO 13485 certification; company states adherence to ICH GMP standards. Source: ArcticZymes company site, host-DNA depletion page — https://www.arcticzymes.com/application/host-cell-dna-depletion-prior-to-diagnostic-metagenomic-analysis (index). No marketed marine-derived drug is claimed.
- **Final buyer / first user:** B2B customers — research, IVD and biomanufacturing firms / CDMOs. Company attributes FY2025 biomanufacturing sales +25% to the M-SAN HQ GMP launch (company claim, **single-source**). No single named pharmaceutical buyer was verified.
- **Guangzhou borrow:** High-value, low-volume enzyme ingredients (not wild harvest) — bioprospecting → recombinant production → ISO 13485/GMP manufacturing. A Guangzhou marine-biotech scenario should be specified as an *ingredient / tool* sale with a named B2B buyer, not as a drug, until a drug candidate exists.

#### C4 — Type 5 Marine data/monitoring: BarentsWatch (national coastal/sea information system)
- **Issuer/offer:** Norwegian Coastal Administration (Kystverket), Centre for the Sharing of Sea and Coast Information, Tromsø; portal barentswatch.no; launched 2012. Partners: 10 ministries + 29 administrative agencies and research institutes.
- **Pays/funding:** **Not verified** (budget/annual cost). Development work "is carried out by commercial suppliers through public procurement" (Kystverket). Source: Kystverket, verified live — https://www.kystverket.no/en/navigation-and-monitoring/barentswatch/
- **Validation/standards:** **Not verified** (named data certifier). Kystverket notes the programme "puts more pressure on standardizing the format of the various data sources" — i.e. standardisation is an outcome, not a pre-existing certificate.
- **Final buyer / first user:** Governments, businesses and individuals; operational users include rescue/emergency services (Shared Resources Register) and shipping (ArcticInfo — free, map-based, ice/weather/AIS, via barentswatch.no). Data are open, with restricted sections for authorised agencies.
- **Guangzhou borrow:** One portal, open APIs, multi-agency data reuse — and *procure the build, keep the data governance public*. This is the closest model to a Guangzhou marine-monitoring-data service that can be sold/traded rather than just published.

### 2.2 Singapore (2 cases)

#### C5 — Type 3 Smart ports/shipping: Tuas Port (Next Generation Port)
- **Issuer/offer:** Maritime and Port Authority of Singapore (MPA) — developer/regulator; PSA International — operator. Officially opened 1 Sep 2022.
- **Pays/funding:** **Not verified** (total project cost / funding split). No total cost figure was verified from a primary source in this run.
- **Validation/standards:** **Not verified** (named automation certifier). Technology is described by MPA/PSA: electrified automated yard cranes, AGVs, private 5G, central control centre, smart grid.
- **Final buyer / first user:** PSA / shipping lines calling at Singapore. Scale (MPA): 65m TEU/yr when complete in the 2040s (almost double 37.5m TEU handled in 2021); Phase 1: 21 deep-water berths, 20m TEU/yr when fully operational in 2027; 12 berths operational as of Dec 2025 (MPA Annual Report 2025). Sources: MPA "Port of the Future" — https://www.mpa.gov.sg/maritime-singapore/port-of-the-future (index); MPA Annual Report 2025 — https://www.mpa.gov.sg/api/media/f520f5e0-28b0-49bc-8785-86134fadfbc8/MPA-AR2025.pdf (index).
- **Guangzhou borrow:** Design automation in from the seabed up (not a retrofit), phase the consolidation of older terminals into one site, and publish a dated berth/TEU ramp (21 berths / 20m TEU by 2027) so progress is auditable. Note the gap: without a published total cost, Tuas is a *design* model, not a *cost* model.

#### C6 — Type 5 Marine data/monitoring: digitalPORT@SG + OCEANS-X
- **Issuer/offer:** MPA. digitalPORT@SG launched 2019 as a maritime single window; digitalOCEANS (2020) for ship–port data-standard harmonisation; OCEANS-X (2022) as an API marketplace.
- **Pays/funding:** **Not verified** (budget). MPA/government funded — amount not stated in the sources read.
- **Validation/standards:** IMO Maritime Single Window alignment; for digital bunkering, Enterprise Singapore / Singapore Standards Council SS 709:2024 and revised SS 648 (index, via e-BDN reporting). OCEANS-X standardises APIs so a new party reuses an existing API instead of building one.
- **Final buyer / first user:** Shipmasters and agents from >550 shipping companies; 16 vessel/immigration/port-health forms consolidated into one application. Claimed saving: 100,000 man-hours/yr (digitalPORT) + up to a further 100,000 man-hours/yr from the port-clearance API. Source: Prime Minister's Office Singapore, DPM Heng Swee Keat, Singapore Maritime Week opening, 2022, verified live — https://www.pmo.gov.sg/newsroom/dpm-heng-swee-keat-at-the-opening-ceremony-of-the-singapore-maritime-week/ . **Single-source** (government speech) for the man-hours figures — treat as a claimed, not independently audited, outcome.
- **Guangzhou borrow:** Single window first (one form, one portal), API marketplace second, just-in-time planning third. The sequence matters: Guangzhou's marine-monitoring data service should first consolidate existing clearances/forms, then open APIs — not launch APIs before the window exists.

### 2.3 United Kingdom (3 cases)

#### C7 — Type 1 Deep-sea equipment/test sites: Smart Sound Plymouth
- **Issuer/offer:** Plymouth Marine Laboratory (PML) operates it; Plymouth is the UK's designated national centre for marine autonomy. Authorised water space via King's Harbour Master Plymouth; research vessel *Plymouth Quest*; Remote Operation Centre; L4 buoy; 5G (incl. underwater connectivity claim).
- **Pays/funding:** Mixed — "Innovate UK funding, research grants or commercial budgets" (Smart Sound site). No single operating budget verified. Named project funding: Smart Sound Connect Subsurface (SSCS), 3 years, £1.2m; Frontline consortium, £3.5m, funded by NERC + The Crown Estate, led by Heriot-Watt University (Marine Insight, 3 Nov 2026, index).
- **Validation/standards:** **Not verified** as a single autonomy certifier. Site claims: MoD Security Clearance, Cyber Essentials Plus, JOSCAR/Aurora/Helios defence-procurement registration; "digital twins & simulations for autonomy assurance & regulation". Source: Smart Sound Plymouth, verified live — https://smartsoundplymouth.co.uk/
- **Final buyer / first user:** Technology developers, startups, academia, defence primes. Named trial users: University of Plymouth / NOC ALR1500 ("Boaty McBoatface") with echosounder/hydrophone/HOLOtag plankton sensor (Frontline trial, Nov 2026); ACUA Ocean, ecoSUB Robotics, Seaber, Sonardyne (SSCS demo). 30+ projects completed; water depth up to 75m; >100 years of Western Channel Observatory data.
- **Guangzhou borrow:** The tradable product is *authorised water + baseline data + a charter vessel + a ROC*, sold as flexible packages by TRL — not the water alone. The 100-year environmental baseline is what makes a test result verifiable; Guangzhou should publish its own baseline (seabed survey) before selling test slots.

#### C8 — Type 2 Offshore wind: Dogger Bank (A–C, AR3; South, AR7)
- **Issuer/offer:** The Crown Estate (seabed lease, Round 3); UK Government DESNZ/BEIS (CfD); Planning Inspectorate (DCO). Developers: SSE Renewables + Equinor (A–C); RWE (51%) + Masdar (49%) (South).
- **Pays/funding:** CfD AR3, 20 Sep 2019 (gov.uk, index): A 1,200MW £39.65/MWh, B 1,200MW £41.61/MWh, C (Teesside A) 1,200MW £41.61/MWh (2012 prices), 15 years — https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/contracts-for-difference-cfd-allocation-round-3-results/contracts-for-difference-cfd-allocation-round-3-results . Dogger Bank South: CfD AR7 Jan 2026, £91.20/MWh (RWE, 14 May 2026, verified live — https://www.rwe.com/en/press/rwe-offshore-wind-gmbh/2026-05-14-rwe-and-masdar-receive-planning-permission-for-dogger-bank-south-offshore-wind-farm/ ). Financing for A–C ~£9bn at financial close (secondary, index) — **single-source / not verified from a primary source**.
- **Validation/standards:** LCCC administers CfDs; milestone requirement (10% spend/commitments) before construction. DCO for South granted May 2026 (Lord Whitehead, on behalf of Energy Secretary). Technical (turbine/foundation) certifier **not verified** from the primary sources read.
- **Final buyer / first user:** UK grid/consumers via CfD + 15-year PPAs; first power from Dogger Bank A 10 Oct 2023. South: 3GW combined, ~3m homes, FID target 2027, commissioning 2031 (West) / 2032 (East).
- **Guangzhou borrow:** The sequence — seabed lease → CfD price auction → DCO → FID — is the replicable "scenario-to-project" pipeline. The strike-price jump from £39.65–41.61 (AR3, 2019) to £91.20 (AR7, 2026) is itself useful evidence: Guangzhou should index its own offshore-wind price expectations, not anchor on 2019 prices.

#### C9 — Type 5 Marine data/monitoring: BODC — Seabed 2030 Global Center (bathymetry)
- **Issuer/offer:** British Oceanographic Data Centre (BODC), part of the National Oceanography Centre (NOC); designated marine science data centre for NERC; origins 1969 (as BODS), BODC formed Apr 1989.
- **Pays/funding:** **Not verified** (NERC/UKRI funding amount; Nippon Foundation–GEBCO Seabed 2030 funding amount). NOC ~650 staff; BODC ~50 staff.
- **Validation/standards:** IOC International Oceanographic Data and Information Exchange (IODE) network (one of 60+ national centres); NERC Environmental Data Service; BODC has maintained/updated/delivered GEBCO global bathymetric products since 1990. Source: Seabed 2030, verified live — https://seabed2030.org/global-center/ ; BODC history — https://www.bodc.ac.uk/about/what_is_bodc/our_history/ (index).
- **Final buyer / first user:** International community via the GEBCO global grid; the four Seabed 2030 Regional Centers feed gridded data to BODC, which produces the centralised seamless global grid and makes it available online. Also: NERC-funded marine projects (data management) and the National Oceanographic Database (NODB).
- **Guangzhou borrow:** A national data centre becomes tradable when it becomes a *global* hub for one product (here, the GEBCO grid). Guangzhou's seabed-survey scenario should name one global product it will feed (e.g. a regional bathymetry grid) and one standard it will meet, rather than promising "data sharing" in general. Note: Seabed 2030's coverage percentage was **not verified** in this run — do not cite a % complete figure.

### 2.4 Japan (2 cases)

#### C10 — Type 3 Smart ports/shipping: MEGURI2040 — GENBU (and 3 sister vessels)
- **Issuer/offer:** The Nippon Foundation launched MEGURI2040 Fully Autonomous Ship Program, Feb 2020; DFFAS+ consortium (53 companies, led by Japan Marine Science). Goal: 50% of Japan's coastal vessels autonomous by 2040 (Foundation goal; government has not adopted it as its own target — index).
- **Pays/funding:** **Not verified** from a primary source. Secondary reporting puts each stage at ~¥10bn with the Foundation providing >80% — **single-source, secondary, not verified**; do not use as a verified figure.
- **Validation/standards:** ClassNK AUTO-NAV2(All) notation 26 Jan 2026 + MLIT inspection as an autonomous vessel (MASS) 28 Jan 2026. MLIT safety standards/inspection methods for unmanned shipping established 2024; 2025 amendments in force 1 Jun 2025. Source: Nippon Foundation, 30 Jan 2026 — https://en.nippon-foundation.or.jp/news/articles/2026/20260130-110050.html (index; URL with tracking parameters in search result — use the clean path above). Also: https://en.nippon-foundation.or.jp/news/articles/2026/20260309-111454.html (9 Mar 2026, Hokuren Maru No.2).
- **Final buyer / first user:** Suzuyo Marine Co. operates GENBU; Ikous Corp. maintains it. GENBU: 134.9m, 5,689 GT, ~700 TEU, regular commercial service from 30 Jan 2026, Kobe–Tokyo via Osaka, Nagoya, Shimizu, Yokohama. Sister vessels: *Olympia Dream Seto* (passenger ferry, commercial service Dec 2025), *Hokuren Maru No.2* (Ro-Ro, Kushiro–Hitachi), *Mikage* (container, certified 25 Mar 2026 per Nippon.com, 24 Apr 2026, index). All four passed MLIT inspection by Mar 2026.
- **Guangzhou borrow:** Four *different* vessel types in parallel (container, Ro-Ro, ferry, container) on real commercial routes — the scenario is validated by *route diversity*, not by one demo ship. And the regulator writes the rule *from* the trial data and shares it with IMO — a Guangzhou smart-shipping scenario should include a rule-making deliverable, not just a vessel.

#### C11 — Type 4 Marine biotech: JAMSTEC Deep-sea Bio Open Innovation Platform
- **Issuer/offer:** Japan Agency for Marine-Earth Science and Technology (JAMSTEC), Research and Development Center for Marine Biosciences / Center for Bioscience and Biotechnology, Yokosuka. Established Sep 2017; trial provision, then full-scale provision Feb 2020. Offers deep-sea sediments, microbial strains and environmental genomic information.
- **Pays/funding:** **Not verified** (platform budget). "Initial cost is kept low" to encourage use; JAMSTEC retains ownership of the resources it provides.
- **Validation/standards:** **Not verified** (named biotech/drug certifier). No GMP/ISO certificate for the platform was found in the sources read.
- **Final buyer / first user:** Domestic private companies, universities and national research institutes — "already provided bioresources to private companies, universities, and national research institutes on a trial basis" (JPMA, 2020). No named company or product was verified. Source: Japan Pharmaceutical Manufacturers Association (JPMA) Newsletter Mar 2020 No.196, press tour 18 Dec 2019, verified live — https://en.jpma.or.jp/news_room/newsletter/196/96t7-01.html ; JAMSTEC history — https://www.jamstec.go.jp/e/about/history/ (index: "Sep 2017 Deep-sea Bio Open Innovation Platform established").
- **Guangzhou borrow:** The most transferable biotech mechanism in this whole set: a *resource library* with clear IP rules (provider keeps the resource, user owns modified materials and IP on derivatives) and a low entry price. Guangzhou could offer its own deep-sea / South China Sea sample/genome library on the same terms — the scenario is the *terms*, not the samples.

### 2.5 South Korea (2 cases)

#### C12 — Type 1 Deep-sea equipment/test sites: KRISO Deep Ocean Engineering Basin (DOEB), Busan
- **Issuer/offer:** Korea Research Institute of Ships and Ocean Engineering (KRISO), affiliated with KIOST; Busan Deep Ocean Engineering Research Center, Saenggok Complex, Gangseo-gu, Busan; completed 29 Nov 2018.
- **Pays/funding:** **Not verified** (construction/operating budget). KRISO is a government institute; no project budget was extracted from the KRISO Annual Report 2025 in this run.
- **Validation/standards:** KRISO itself performs "performance evaluation and verification of ships and offshore structures through reproduction of the deep sea environment". A named external certifier for DOEB tests was **not verified**. (KRISO Annual Report 2025 lists ISO 9001:2015 in the facilities table — the table's layout does not clearly attribute it to the DOEB alone, so it is **not** claimed here for the DOEB.)
- **Final buyer / first user:** Korean shipbuilding / offshore-plant industry. Named use: buoyancy-engine test module for underwater gliders — tested in the 15m pit, then in the 50m deep pit, then at sea (East Sea, ~110m, dive to 100m) (Journal of Ocean Engineering and Technology, 2022, index). Tank: 100m × 50m × 15m; deep pit 12m diameter × 50m depth (KRISO Annual Report 2025, index; KRISO overview, verified live — https://www.kriso.re.kr/menu.es?mid=a20102000000 — "Water tank: 100x50x15 m, Deep sea pit: 50 m").
- **Note on figures:** A 2022 journal paper describes max depth as 50m (15m + 30m) in one passage and 15m + deep pit in another; KRISO's own overview/annual report give 100×50×15m + 50m pit. Use KRISO's own figures; flag the journal-paper wording as inconsistent.
- **Guangzhou borrow:** This is the direct template for Guangzhou's deep-pit test site: the world's largest deep-ocean basin *plus* a 50m pit, with a named test sequence (basin pit → sea trial). Guangzhou should publish the same three numbers (tank L×W×D, pit diameter × depth, and the basin-to-sea test sequence) and price test slots by TRL — KRISO's missing piece (no published price list was found) is a gap Guangzhou can fill first.

#### C13 — Type 4 Marine biotech: KIOST *Thermococcus onnurineus* NA1 — biohydrogen demo
- **Issuer/offer:** Korea Institute of Ocean Science and Technology (KIOST), Marine Biotechnology Research Center, Busan. Strain isolated 2002 by R/V *Onnuri* at 1,650m, South Pacific hydrothermal vent; first marine extremophile isolated/cultivated/named by Korea.
- **Pays/funding:** **Not verified** (demo-plant cost). Later work was funded by MOF project 20210639 ("Development of Biohydrogen Plant Operation Optimization System") and NRF C1 Gas Refinery Program (Ministry of Science and ICT) — amounts **not verified**.
- **Validation/standards:** **Not verified** (named certifier / hydrogen purity standard).
- **Final buyer / first user:** **Not verified** (commercial buyer). Demo only: 1-ton fermenter at Dangjin Steel Mill (2016); 50-ton fermenter demo plant at Taean Seobu Power Plant since 2017, managed by Kyungdong Engineering; claimed output 330t H2/yr, enough for 1,000–2,000 hydrogen cars/yr. Source: Donga Science, 29 Nov 2019 — https://www.dongascience.com/en/news/32645 (index). **Single-source** for the demo scale/outcome; no post-2019 commercial outcome was verified. KIOST confirms the strain is "now being used in research on biohydrogen pilot-scale production" (KIOST press, Dec 2025, index).
- **Guangzhou borrow:** Staged scale-up (lab → 1-ton → 50-ton at a power plant, using industrial by-product gas as feedstock) is the right scenario shape — but Guangzhou should require a *named off-taker* for the hydrogen before funding the 50-ton stage, which this Korean case does not demonstrate.

### 2.6 Netherlands (2 cases)

#### C14 — Type 1 Deep-sea equipment/test sites: MARIN Offshore Basin, Wageningen
- **Issuer/offer:** Maritime Research Institute Netherlands (MARIN), Wageningen; serves shipbuilding, offshore industry and governments; ~450 employees (2022), 9 test facilities.
- **Pays/funding:** **Not verified** (client fees / project funding). MARIN is a commercial test service; no price list or grant amount was verified in this run.
- **Validation/standards:** MARIN itself is the validator (model tests, simulation, full-scale measurements). Basin: 45m × 36m × 10.2m; pit extra 20m depth, 5m diameter, for systems up to 3,000m prototype depth; models 3–6m; wind/wave/current combined; movable floor 36×31m. Source: MARIN, verified live — https://www.marin.nl/en/about/facilities-and-tools/basins/offshore-basin . A named external certifier that accepts MARIN results was **not verified**.
- **Final buyer / first user:** Offshore-wind / offshore developers who commission model tests. Named examples: DeepCwind 1:50 semi-submersible tested at MARIN ocean basin 2013 (OC5/IEA Wind Task 30, NREL — index); FLOTANT project (EU) — scaled hybrid floating platform tested at MARIN basin for global performance under controlled conditions (MARIN, index — https://www.marin.nl/en/jips/flotant ).
- **Guangzhou borrow:** A deep pit does not need to be the largest to be tradable — MARIN's pit is only 5m diameter × 20m extra depth, yet it sells tests for 3,000m prototype systems by Froude scaling. Guangzhou should sell *scaling + combined wind/wave/current* as the service, and publish the scaling rules, rather than competing on pit size alone.

#### C15 — Type 2 Offshore wind: Borssele III–IV (Blauwwind), Dutch North Sea
- **Issuer/offer:** Netherlands Enterprise Agency (RVO) — permits, site studies, tenders; Ministry of Economic Affairs (Minister Kamp). Grid: state TSO TenneT.
- **Pays/funding:** SDE+ subsidy auction. Winning bid 5.45 €c/kWh; subsidy €0.3bn vs €5bn originally budgeted; 26 bids from 7 parties/consortia; maximum subsidy capped at 11.975 €c/kWh; grid connection excluded (TenneT). Source: Blauwwind, 12 Jun 2016, verified live (crawled 2025-11-14) — https://www.blauwwind.nl/en/news/2020/3/dutch-consortium-borssele ; RVO Dutch Offshore Wind Innovation Guide 2026 (index).
- **Validation/standards:** RVO Site Decisions III, IV & V became irrevocable 21 May 2016 (no appeals). Technical certifier **not verified**.
- **Final buyer / first user:** Dutch electricity market/consumers. Consortium Blauwwind (originally Shell, Eneco, Partners Group, Mitsubishi, Van Oord; later Shell/Eneco/INPEX/Luxcara/Swiss Life per RVO guide). 731.5MW, 77 × Vestas 9.5MW turbines. Commissioned 2020 (Blauwwind/RVO guide) — one secondary table gives 2021; flagged discrepancy.
- **Guangzhou borrow:** The state does the expensive, common work (site studies, permits, grid) and auctions only the price — competition then collapses the subsidy (€5bn → €0.3bn). For Guangzhou offshore wind, the replicable move is *government-funded site investigation + grid, price-only auction*, not a larger subsidy.

## 4. What Guangzhou could borrow — summary by Guangzhou's 2027 projects

- **Deep-pit test site:** KRISO (100×50×15m + 12m×50m pit, basin→sea sequence) + MARIN (sell scaling + combined wind/wave/current, publish scaling rules). Publish tank/pit dimensions, TRL packages and a price list — neither KRISO nor MARIN published a price list in this run.
- **Seabed survey:** BODC/Seabed 2030 (feed one global product, meet one standard) + Smart Sound (publish a 100-year-style baseline *before* selling test slots) + BarentsWatch (open APIs, multi-agency reuse).
- **Offshore wind:** Borssele (state does site studies + grid, price-only auction) + Dogger Bank (lease → CfD → DCO → FID sequence; index prices, do not anchor on 2019) + Hywind Tampen (captive first buyer for floating).
- **Marine monitoring data:** BarentsWatch (one portal, procured build, public data governance) + digitalPORT@SG (single window first, API marketplace second).
- **Smart ports:** Tuas (automation designed in, phased berth/TEU ramp) + Yara Birkeland (owner-as-first-user, staged autonomy, shore centre) + MEGURI2040 (four vessel types in parallel, regulator writes rules from trial data) + Busan Dongwon (supplementary: first fully automated terminal Apr 2024, KRW 200bn expansion to 2028).
